#  Concept Note: The Physical Environment & Substrate Requirements (Freshney)

##  Objective
Examining how the physical interface between cultured cells and their container—alongside atmospheric control—dictates cell attachment, spreading, polarization, and differentiation.

## Key notes
* **Surface Chemistry & Hydrophilicity:** Untreated polystyrene is hydrophobic. Corona discharge or gas plasma treatment introduces charged oxygen-containing hydrophilic groups, permitting protein adsorption from serum and subsequent cell adhesion via integrin receptors.
* **Anoikis Prevention:** Normal somatic cells require physical anchorage to a solid substratum to prevent *anoikis* (apoptosis induced by inadequate cell-matrix interactions), whereas transformed or suspension-adapted lines proliferate independently.
* **Gaseous Microenvironment:** The critical balance between ambient air, $\text{CO}_2$ (typically 5% for bicarbonate-buffered media), and water vapor saturation. Inadequate humidity leads to rapid media evaporation, causing hyperosmolality and localized salt precipitation.

## ⚠ Troubleshooting
* **Poor Cell Adhesion / Round Morphology:** 
  * *Root Cause:* Using non-tissue-culture treated plasticware (suspension dishes) or expired/damaged surface-treated plates.
  * *Correction:* Ensure plates are explicitly marked as "tissue-culture treated" (TC-treated) or manually apply ECM coatings (collagen, fibronectin, or poly-L-lysine).
* **Hyperosmolality & Salt Precipitation:** 
  * *Root Cause:* Inadequate incubator humidity causing rapid evaporation from culture vessels.
  * *Correction:* Check water pan levels in the incubator and ensure lids on culture dishes permit proper gas exchange without excessive draft.
